How to read these scores

General EP Fit Assessment reports two constructs — Critical Thinking and Emotional Intelligence — each measured two independent ways:

  • Trait (e.g. CT-Trait, EQ-Trait) — from a set of forced-choice comparison questions. The applicant is shown four statements about themselves and picks which is most and least like them. This measures how they see their own tendencies.
  • Scenario (e.g. CT-Scenario, EQ-Scenario) — from realistic "what would you do" situations specific to executive protection work. This measures applied judgment in the moment, not self-description.
  • Blended (Crit.Think. / EQ) — the Trait and Scenario scores for that construct, averaged. This is the headline number.

A large gap between an applicant's Trait and Scenario score on the same construct is worth asking about in interview — rows with a gap of 30+ points are flagged automatically below. It usually means one of two things: they can identify the right answer in a clear scenario but don't see that quality as central to who they are, or the reverse. Either is worth a direct follow-up question, not a red flag on its own.

Estate / Event / Driving Security Scenarios report a single Judgment Score — the percentage of points earned across that track's 5 role-specific situational questions.

Band meaning: Strong Signal (70–100) reflects consistently sound judgment across the measured items. Mixed Signal (45–69) is where interview probing matters most. Limited Signal (0–44) — prioritize related interview questions for that applicant.

All of this is a decision-support input, not a pass/fail cutoff — use it alongside the structured interview and situational judgment scoring.
